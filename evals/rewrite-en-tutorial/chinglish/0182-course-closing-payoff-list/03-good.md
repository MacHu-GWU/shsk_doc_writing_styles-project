By the time you finish this course, GitHub won't feel like some unfamiliar programmer's tool anymore. It'll be your default home for everyday collaboration and version control. You can create a repo on your own, edit files, and get work done with branches and PRs. You know what belongs in public and what should stay private, you can bring the right people in to collaborate, and you can let a program act on your behalf with a token. Working on your own machine in GitHub Desktop feels natural, and you can tell at a glance who a repository belongs to and where to go looking for it. As your own main workspace, that's plenty.
