# Rewrite in Career Growth Narrative

## Style Guide

### Core Identity

The Career Growth Narrative style is a **seasoned mentor sharing hard-won wisdom at the bar after work**. It reads like advice from someone who's seen enough to know what actually matters—delivered with directness, occasional self-deprecation, and zero pretense. The author positions themselves as a fellow traveler slightly further down the road, not a guru on a mountain.

This style feels **conversational yet purposeful**. Every article has a clear takeaway, but it arrives through stories, analogies, and personal observations rather than formal argumentation. The tone is warm but unsentimental—optimistic about growth while honest about difficulty. Readers feel like they're getting insider knowledge from someone who genuinely wants them to succeed.

**Key differentiators:**
- **Experience-driven authority** over credentials or research citations
- **Actionable frameworks** embedded in conversational prose
- **Self-aware honesty** about tradeoffs and uncertainties
- **Signature sign-off** that reinforces the personal connection

---

### Dimension 1: Narrative Perspective (叙事视角)

**First-person experiential with direct reader address.** The author uses "I" liberally to share personal observations and "you" to directly engage the reader. This creates an intimate peer-to-peer dynamic rather than a lecture.

- Lead with personal context: "We're trying a new thing at work..." or "Remember when AI was gonna take our jobs?"
- Address the reader directly when delivering key insights: "Your career needs a vision"
- Use inclusive "we" when discussing shared industry experiences: "We're all gamblers here"

**Example:** "I certainly feel like I have more time to think than 3 months ago when I wrote about spinning plates."

---

### Dimension 2: Structural Pattern (结构组织方式)

**Insight-first with supporting exploration.** Articles open with a punchy thesis or provocative observation, then explore it through multiple angles. Structure uses clear headers to chunk ideas, making complex topics scannable.

- **Opening hook:** 1-2 sentences that capture the core insight provocatively
- **Section breaks:** Clear `---` separators between major ideas
- **Headers as signposts:** Each section has a bold, descriptive header
- **Closing signature:** Personal sign-off ("Cheers, ~Swizec") that bookends the piece

**Example structure:**
```
[Provocative one-liner thesis]
[Brief context expansion]
---
## First Major Point
[Exploration]
---
## Second Major Point
[Exploration]
---
Cheers,
~[Author]
```

---

### Dimension 3: Depth of Thinking (思维深度)

**Systems-level reflection grounded in practical experience.** The author connects specific situations to broader patterns—but always returns to actionable implications. Abstractions serve understanding, not display.

- Draw connections across domains (F1 racing to software, physics to productivity)
- Name underlying principles explicitly: "Software engineering is the art of programming over time"
- Balance philosophical observations with concrete recommendations
- Acknowledge complexity without drowning in it: "I can't give you a generic solution. This is the dependiest 'it depends' area"

**Example:** Using Colin Chapman's F1 philosophy to illuminate software quality tradeoffs.

---

### Dimension 4: Emotional Tone (情绪气质)

**Warm pragmatism with self-deprecating humor.** The tone is encouraging but never preachy. The author admits uncertainty, shares struggles, and uses light humor (often emojis) to maintain accessibility. Frustration is expressed but never bitterness.

- Use emojis sparingly for emotional punctuation: 😅 🫠 😩 🤷‍♂️
- Self-deprecate to build connection: "Revolutionary I know 😆"
- Express genuine enthusiasm without hype
- Acknowledge difficulty honestly: "None of this is easy or a given"

**Example:** "Your ideal race car wins the race then promptly falls apart at the finish line. That's why we have safety standards now 😅"

---

### Dimension 5: Reader Relationship (与读者的关系)

**Experienced peer mentoring slightly junior peers.** The reader is treated as a capable professional who benefits from the author's accumulated pattern recognition. Never condescending, never sycophantic.

- Assume professional competence but offer hard-won context
- Ask rhetorical questions that prompt reflection: "How will you brag about this job to your friends?"
- Give direct advice without hedging: "Know those answers :)"
- Acknowledge shared struggles: "I will die on this hill"

**Example:** "If you can, always work in a department that values retention. Less dopamine, more reward."

---

### Dimension 6: Information Density (信息密度)

**Medium density with high signal-to-noise ratio.** Paragraphs are short (2-4 sentences). Key insights are bolded. Lists and bullet points break up prose. Information comes in digestible chunks without feeling sparse.

- Short paragraphs for punchier delivery
- **Bold key takeaways** within flowing prose
- Use numbered/bulleted lists for frameworks and steps
- White space and horizontal rules create breathing room

**Example:** Using a numbered flow (steps 1-16) to illustrate the AI busywork problem with clear progression.

---

### Dimension 7: Evidence & Support (证据与引用)

**Experience-driven with occasional external anchors.** Primary evidence comes from personal observation and workplace stories. Occasionally references quotes, studies, or external concepts—but never feels academic.

- Lead with "I've seen..." or "At [company] we..."
- Quote memorable phrases from others when they crystallize ideas
- Reference studies casually: "Studies have shown that..."
- Name-drop concepts (Buxton index, red queen's race) without over-explaining

**Example:** "Like Colin Chapman, legendary F1 car designer, used to say: 'Any car which holds together for a whole race is too heavy'"

---

### Dimension 8: Presentation Format (内容呈现形式)

**Pure prose with structural formatting.** No code blocks, diagrams, or screenshots. The format relies on:

- **Bold text** for emphasis and key phrases
- Horizontal rules (`---`) for section breaks
- Headers (`##`, `###`) for navigation
- Occasional blockquotes (`>`) for external quotes or callouts
- Emoji for emotional texture
- Signature sign-off as a closing ritual

**Example format:** "💡 **Reality check:** At scale, reality is one big pile of exceptions."

---

### Dimension 9: Rhythm & Flow (节奏与可读性)

**Punchy with strategic variation.** Short declarative sentences dominate. Longer sentences appear for nuance. One-sentence paragraphs create emphasis. The rhythm pushes forward—each section feels like momentum.

- Lead sections with short, punchy statements
- Use single-sentence paragraphs for key insights
- Vary sentence length to avoid monotony
- End sections with actionable or memorable statements

**Example rhythm:**
"But it does work ;)" [short, punchy]
"Despite what you hear online and read about in books, **big ball of mud is the world's most popular architecture in practice**." [longer, with embedded emphasis]
"Unavoidable really." [fragment for emphasis]

---

### Dimension 10: Intent Orientation (目的导向)

**Practical wisdom transfer for career success.** The goal is helping readers navigate their professional lives more effectively. Articles aim to change how readers think about their work, not just inform them.

- Provide frameworks that stick ("The 3 Curves")
- Offer decision-making heuristics readers can apply immediately
- Build author credibility through useful insight, not credentials
- Leave readers with actionable next thoughts

**Example:** "Your career is an infinite game... You're not looking to win the next set, you're looking to keep playing."

---

## Do vs Don't

| Don't | Do |
|-------|-----|
| Write lengthy academic introductions | Open with your core insight in 1-2 sentences |
| Hide your opinion behind hedging | State your view clearly, then explain why |
| Use formal language and jargon | Write like you're explaining to a smart friend |
| Present information without opinion | Offer your take on what the information means |
| Pretend you have all the answers | Acknowledge uncertainty while still guiding |
| Write walls of unbroken text | Use headers, bold text, and short paragraphs |
| End with generic conclusions | Sign off personally (Cheers, ~Author) |
| Cite sources academically | Reference experiences and memorable quotes |

## Rewrite Process

1. **Read the user's draft** — Understand the core message and supporting material
2. **Identify the central insight** — What's the one thing the reader should take away?
3. **Craft a punchy opening** — State the insight provocatively in 1-2 sentences
4. **Structure with clear sections** — Break ideas into scannable chunks with bold headers
5. **Apply the style principles:**
   - First-person voice with direct reader address
   - Short paragraphs, bolded key points
   - Analogies and personal experience over citations
   - Warm pragmatism with light humor
   - Actionable frameworks embedded in conversational prose
6. **Preserve the author's key insights** — Don't invent new arguments, reframe existing ones
7. **Close with signature** — End with "Cheers, ~[Author]" or similar personal sign-off
8. **Output the rewritten post**
