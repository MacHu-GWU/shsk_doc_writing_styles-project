# Rewrite in Analytical Deep Dive Essay Style

## Style Guide

### Core Identity

This style feels like reading a carefully constructed legal brief or academic argument written for intelligent laypeople. The writer takes a position and methodically builds a case, anticipating objections and addressing them systematically. Reading it feels like watching someone dismantle conventional wisdom with precision—not through rhetoric, but through accumulated evidence and clear reasoning.

The key differentiator: where other styles might share experiences or explain concepts, this style **argues**. It has a thesis. It marshals evidence. It considers counterarguments seriously before showing why they fail. The reader finishes feeling not just informed, but convinced—or at minimum, forced to reconsider their priors.

### Dimension Guidelines

#### 1. Narrative Perspective (叙事视角)
Use third-person analytical perspective as the default. Insert first-person only for personal anecdotes that serve as evidence ("I experienced this...") or to acknowledge epistemic limits ("I don't know enough about X to judge..."). Address the reader with "you" sparingly and deliberately—usually to invite them to consider something specific.

*Example:* "Consider what actually happens when..." rather than "I think you should consider..."

#### 2. Structural Pattern (结构组织方式)
Build arguments hierarchically with clear H2/H3 headers that telegraph the argument's shape. Each section should advance the thesis, not just cover a topic. Use this pattern: **Establish the problem → Present the conventional response → Show why that fails → Offer a deeper analysis → Anticipate objections → Conclude with implications.**

Use analogies as structural tools—take a complex concept and map it to something concrete and familiar to make the abstract tangible.

*Example:* Using a mechanic appointment analogy to illuminate AI bias issues makes the abstract concrete.

#### 3. Depth of Thinking (思维深度)
Go beyond "what" to "why it matters" and "what this reveals about how we think." Connect specific examples to system-level patterns. Challenge not just conclusions but the reasoning frameworks people use to reach those conclusions. Show when common solutions fail by examining the actual mechanism of failure.

*Example:* Don't just say diversity hiring doesn't fix bias—show the five levels at which it fails, from incentive misalignment to fundamental software methodology problems.

#### 4. Emotional Tone (情绪气质)
Maintain calm precision with occasional dry wit. Be confident but not aggressive. Express genuine intellectual frustration through understated observation rather than exclamation. Acknowledge uncertainty honestly—this paradoxically increases credibility on points where you are certain.

*Example:* "This is a genuine question, not rhetorical: How hard technically is it to improve the situation?"

#### 5. Reader Relationship (与读者的关系)
Treat readers as intelligent peers who can follow complex arguments without hand-holding. Assume they may disagree but will engage fairly with evidence. Don't explain basics unless necessary for the argument. Invite consideration rather than demand acceptance.

*Example:* "I recommend playing it before reading further" trusts the reader to engage actively.

#### 6. Information Density (信息密度)
High density—pack substantial evidence and reasoning into each section. Use long paragraphs when building complex arguments, but break with short punchy statements for emphasis. External references strengthen claims. Real numbers, percentages, and specifics trump generalities.

*Example:* "36,902 responses produced 9,432 distinct opinions—an average of ~3.9 people per unique position."

#### 7. Evidence & Support (证据与引用)
Combine data, case studies, and logical analysis. Cite specific numbers and sources. Test claims empirically when possible. Use quotes from others to demonstrate real-world examples of reasoning patterns you're analyzing. Build evidence cumulatively—each point reinforces the overall thesis.

*Example:* Reproducing AI bias experiments with specific prompts and documenting results strengthens claims beyond mere assertion.

#### 8. Presentation Format (内容呈现形式)
Text-heavy prose with strategic use of:
- Bullet points for lists of examples or enumerated arguments
- Bold text for key claims and emphasis
- Block quotes for cited material
- Headers to structure the argument's flow
- Occasional numbered lists for sequential reasoning

No code unless directly relevant. No images unless they provide evidence.

#### 9. Rhythm & Flow (节奏与可读性)
Vary sentence length deliberately. Use short, punchy sentences to land key points after longer explanatory passages. Bold text highlights crucial insights. Create "push forward" momentum by ending sections with implications that lead naturally to the next section.

*Example:* "Yet this is precisely the argument people make with AI." (Short, landing the point after a longer passage.)

#### 10. Intent Orientation (目的导向)
Aim to change how readers think about a topic, not just inform them. Challenge assumptions readers didn't know they held. Be definitive—take positions and defend them. The goal is intellectual influence: after reading, the reader should find it harder to accept the conventional view uncritically.

### Do vs Don't

| Don't | Do |
|-------|-----|
| State opinions without evidence | Support every claim with specific evidence, data, or logical argument |
| Accept conventional wisdom at face value | Question common assumptions and show where they fail empirically |
| Use vague language ("many people," "often") | Use specific numbers and concrete examples ("88.3% of people disagreed") |
| Dismiss opposing views without engaging | Steel-man counterarguments, then show specifically why they fail |
| End sections without advancing the argument | Each section should move toward the thesis—avoid standalone "interesting observations" |
| Write in a casual, chatty tone | Maintain analytical precision while staying readable |
| Rely on emotional appeals | Let accumulated evidence create conviction |
| Present single examples as proof | Build cases with multiple independent lines of evidence |

## Rewrite Process

1. **Read the user's draft** — Identify the core claim or argument being made
2. **Formulate a clear thesis** — What is the draft actually arguing? Sharpen it into a defensible position
3. **Identify evidence and materials** — What data, examples, and reasoning does the draft provide? What's missing?
4. **Structure the argument** — Arrange material into: Problem → Conventional View → Why That Fails → Deeper Analysis → Implications
5. **Apply style guide principles** — Use analytical perspective, high information density, specific evidence
6. **Add counterargument handling** — What would skeptics say? Address it explicitly
7. **Preserve the author's key insights** — The rewrite should amplify, not replace, the author's original thinking
8. **Output the rewritten post** — Complete, publication-ready

## Style Markers Checklist

Before finalizing, verify the rewrite has:
- [ ] A clear, arguable thesis stated early
- [ ] Hierarchical structure with meaningful headers
- [ ] Specific numbers, data, or concrete examples
- [ ] At least one counterargument addressed
- [ ] System-level thinking (beyond surface observations)
- [ ] Varied sentence rhythm with short punchy statements for emphasis
- [ ] Calm, precise tone throughout
- [ ] Conclusion with broader implications
